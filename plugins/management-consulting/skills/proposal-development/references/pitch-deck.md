# Pitch Deck: Full Template and Craft

Load this when the user is building a pitch deck (Stage 5) and needs the slide-by-slide structure, slide-development guidance, or storytelling rules. The SKILL.md body carries the core arc and the two override principles. This file holds the detail.

## Determine deck objectives first

Clarify before building a single slide:

- **Audience**: C-suite, board, or working team. Each gets different treatment.
- **Occasion**: initial pitch, follow-up, final presentation, or oral defense.
- **Time available**: this drives slide count and depth more than anything else.
- **Key messages**: what should they remember after the room clears?
- **Desired action**: what do we want them to do next?

## Standard structure (15-20 slides)

```
Opening (1-2 slides)
  1. Title

The Challenge (2-3 slides)
  2. Context and situation
  3. The problem / opportunity
  4. Why now?

Our Solution (4-6 slides)
  5. Our approach
  6. Methodology
  7. Key differentiators
  8. What makes us unique

Results & Credentials (3-4 slides)
  9. Relevant case study 1
  10. Relevant case study 2
  11. Team experience

The Deal (2-3 slides)
  12. Scope and approach
  13. Investment
  14. Timeline
  15. Next steps

Closing (1-2 slides)
  16. Summary
  17. Q&A
```

Adjust length to the slot. A 30-minute meeting with real discussion wants 10-12 slides. A detailed capabilities session may need 25 or more. Cut before you pad.

## Slide development by section

**The Challenge** creates tension. Show you live in their world:

- Context: industry trends, market forces, their current position.
- The problem: current state versus desired state. Make the gap visceral, not abstract.
- Why now: market timing, competitive pressure, the cost of doing nothing.

**Our Solution** resolves the tension:

- Approach: the phased methodology, framed as the answer to their specific challenge.
- Differentiators: presented as evidence, not assertion.

| Capability | Our difference | Proof |
|-----------|----------------|-------|
| [Area] | [What sets us apart] | [Evidence, quantified] |

**Results & Credentials** builds belief:

- Case studies as Challenge to Approach to Results (quantified). Add a client testimonial if one exists. Use real firm cases or mark placeholders clearly.
- Team: relevant experience and combined track record, tied to this engagement.

**The Deal** makes it concrete:

- Scope (in and out), investment by phase, timeline with milestones, and a single clear next step.

## Storytelling principles

1. **One message per slide.** Anchor every element to the slide's thesis. If two ideas fight for the headline, split the slide.
2. **Start with why.** Build urgency before you present the solution. A solution with no felt problem lands flat.
3. **Quantify.** Facts build credibility. Label any illustrative figure and flag it for validation.
4. **Create tension, then resolve it.** Problem, solution, result. That arc carries the room.
5. **End with action.** Specific, time-bound next steps. Not a menu of options.
6. **Stay on the storyline.** If a slide does not advance the argument, cut it.
7. **Answer first.** Lead with the recommendation, support with the analysis.
8. **Design for the room.** White space, clarity over density, visuals over walls of text.

## Content integrity on decks

Do not fabricate case results, testimonials, or competitor claims to fill a slide. When a proof point is missing, mark it as a placeholder for the team to supply real data, and frame outcome claims conditionally until then.
