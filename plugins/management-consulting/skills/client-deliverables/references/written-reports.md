# Written Reports

Load this when the deliverable is a document. The core SKILL.md carries the shared logic (SCQA/SCR, the pyramid, action headlines, audience adaptation, writing standards). This file carries report-specific structure: types, length calibration, section templates, the PE IC memo, document assembly, and multi-author coordination.

## Report Types

| Type | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Strategic assessment | Evaluate options and recommend a direction |
| Due diligence report | Investigate and validate claims or assumptions |
| Implementation plan | Define how to execute a strategy |
| Performance review | Assess results against targets |
| Recommendations document | Present findings and proposed actions |
| Status report | Communicate progress and issues |
| PE investment committee memo | Present an investment thesis for a fund decision |

## Depth and Length

Match depth to audience and purpose. Not every analysis needs 30 pages.

| Level | Word Count | When |
|---|---|---|
| Executive brief | 500-800 | Board summary, decision memo, time-pressed stakeholders |
| Working report | 2,000-4,000 | Standard client deliverable with supporting analysis |
| Deep-dive report | 4,000-8,000 | Comprehensive assessment, due diligence, regulatory submission |
| Full study | 8,000+ | Multi-workstream transformation, market-entry study with appendices |

When in doubt, write shorter. A 3,000-word report that gets read beats a 10,000-word report that does not.

## Narrative Structure

Reports use full SCQA: Situation, Complication, Question, Answer. For readers who want to cut to the chase, run answer-first:

1. **Executive summary**: the full message on one page. A reader who stops here still gets the complete picture.
2. **The question**: what we were asked to address.
3. **The answer**: the recommendation in brief.
4. **Supporting evidence**: the analysis, section by section.
5. **Required actions**: what happens, by whom, by when.
6. **Appendices**: supporting detail.

Tone is a choice, not an accident. Set voice (expert, collaborative, or direct) to match the client relationship, technical level to match the reader, and format (formal report, briefing, or working document) to match the setting.

## Section Templates

### Executive Summary

The most important section. It must stand alone. Write it last, after the analysis is done.

- **Bottom line**: one paragraph with the answer, the impact, and the recommendation.
- **Key findings**: 3-5, quantified where the data allows.
- **Recommendations**: prioritized.
- **Expected outcomes**: impact and timeline for each.
- **Next steps**: the immediate actions.

### Analysis Sections

Each one runs the same pattern:

- **Headline**: one sentence capturing the finding, not a topic label.
- **Evidence**: data points with sources and implications.
- **Analysis**: what the evidence means.
- **Implications**: what it means for the business.

When you fill these, write 2-3 real sentences of reasoning and evidence per element. One-word placeholders produce an empty template, not a deliverable.

### Recommendation Sections

Each recommendation needs:

- **The recommendation**: a clear statement of what to do.
- **Rationale**: why this is the right course.
- **Evidence**: the data that backs it.
- **Impact**: projected outcomes, current vs. projected metrics.
- **Risks and mitigations**: what could go wrong and how you address it.

### Scenario Structure (for recommendations under uncertainty)

Structure each scenario explicitly, then state the break-even and the triggers.

| Element | Base Case | Upside | Downside |
|---|---|---|---|
| Key assumption | [Most likely] | [Optimistic] | [Conservative] |
| Revenue / benefit impact | $X | $Y | $Z |
| Probability weighting | 50-60% | 20-25% | 20-25% |
| Trigger indicators | [What you would see] | [What you would see] | [What you would see] |

Add:

- **Decision triggers**: "If [metric] falls below [threshold] by [date], shift to [alternative]."
- **Sensitivity**: which 2-3 assumptions move the outcome most, and what happens at +/- 20%.
- **Break-even**: the point at which the recommendation no longer holds.

Probability weightings assigned without hard data are directional estimates. Say so where you use them.

## PE Investment Committee Memo

IC memos follow a rigid structure because the committee reviews several deals per meeting. Deviating from the expected format slows the decision.

```
INVESTMENT COMMITTEE MEMORANDUM

Company:          [Target Name]
Sector:           [Industry / Sub-sector]
Deal Type:        [Buyout / Growth Equity / Add-on / Recapitalization]
Enterprise Value: [Amount]
Equity Check:     [Amount]
Target Close:     [Date]
Sponsor Coverage: [Deal Team Lead]

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
   - Investment thesis in 3-4 sentences
   - Key return drivers
   - Headline returns: [X.X]x MOIC / [XX]% IRR over [X] year hold

2. COMPANY OVERVIEW
   - Business description, products/services, end markets
   - Revenue: $[X]M | EBITDA: $[X]M | EBITDA Margin: [X]%
   - Revenue mix (product, geography, customer)
   - Customer concentration (top 10 as % of revenue)
   - Management team assessment

3. INVESTMENT THESIS
   - Pillar 1: [Growth lever, quantified]
   - Pillar 2: [Margin lever, quantified]
   - Pillar 3: [Strategic lever, quantified]

4. MARKET & COMPETITIVE DYNAMICS
   - TAM / SAM sizing
   - Growth drivers and secular trends
   - Competitive positioning and moat

5. FINANCIAL OVERVIEW & PROJECTIONS
   - Historical financials (3 years) with key metrics
   - Management case vs. sponsor case
   - Revenue bridge and EBITDA bridge
   - Working capital and capex requirements

6. DEAL STRUCTURE & RETURNS
   - Sources & uses
   - Capital structure and leverage ratios
   - Returns: base / upside / downside
   - Key assumptions per scenario

7. KEY RISKS & MITIGANTS
   | Risk | Severity | Mitigant |
   |------|----------|----------|
   | [Risk 1] | [H/M/L] | [Specific mitigation] |

8. VALUE CREATION PLAN
   - 100-day priorities
   - Operational initiatives with timeline
   - M&A / add-on pipeline (if applicable)

9. EXIT CONSIDERATIONS
   - Likely exit paths (strategic sale, sponsor-to-sponsor, IPO)
   - Comparable exit multiples
   - Target exit timeline

10. RECOMMENDATION
    - Approve / Approve with conditions / Decline
    - Conditions or open items
    - Requested next steps
```

Write it factual, not promotional. The committee's job is to find reasons not to invest, so anticipate their objections and answer them in the memo. Management cases run optimistic; show the sponsor case on conservative assumptions. Every number carries a source.

## Multi-Author Coordination

Large engagements mean multiple contributors. Committee-written outlines produce committee-quality reports, so set these before anyone writes.

**Before writing:**

- **Single outline owner**: one person owns the narrative arc and section structure.
- **Section assignments**: each section has exactly one author. Shared ownership means nobody owns it.
- **Style guide**: agree on voice ("we" vs. third person), tense, headings, and citation format up front.
- **Placeholder convention**: use `[TBD: needed content]` for pending sections. Never leave a section blank.

**During writing:**

- **Integration checkpoints**: review after first drafts and again after revisions. Do not wait for final assembly to find inconsistencies.
- **Cross-reference log**: track which sections cite data from others, so a change in one flags the dependents.
- **Terminology register**: keep a short list of key terms and their agreed use. "Revenue" vs. "net revenue" vs. "ARR" drift across sections destroys credibility.

**Version control and review:**

Naming: `[Client]_[Report]_v[Major].[Minor]_[Date]_[Author].docx`. Major version for structural change or post-review rewrite (v1.0, v2.0); minor for edits inside the existing structure (v1.1). Example: `Acme_MarketEntry_v2.1_2026-03-15_JSmith.docx`.

| Stage | Version | Reviewer | Checks |
|---|---|---|---|
| First draft | v0.1 | Author self-review | Structure, completeness, argument logic |
| Peer review | v0.5 | One colleague | Logic, evidence gaps, readability |
| Manager review | v1.0 | Engagement manager | Narrative arc, client-readiness, accuracy |
| Partner review | v1.5 | Partner / director | Strategic positioning, risk, tone |
| Client draft | v2.0 | Client | Factual accuracy, alignment with expectations |
| Final | v3.0 | Final QA | Formatting, cross-references, typos |

One reviewer at a time. Resolve all comments before passing to the next reviewer; parallel reviews at the same level produce conflicting edits. Lock the shared file when editing, and keep a plain status line: "v1.2 with JSmith for manager review, due back Mar 18."

**Final assembly:** one person does the assembly and voice harmonization. It takes longer than people expect (budget 1-2 days for a full report). Confirm the executive summary reflects the final content, not an earlier draft's conclusions, and verify every cross-reference and page number.

## Report Quality Checklist

On top of the common checklist in SKILL.md:

- Page numbers present.
- Table of contents accurate.
- Grammar and spelling clean.
- Scenario analysis included for any recommendation with real uncertainty.
